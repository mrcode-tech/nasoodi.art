The studio has been full of unfinished conversations between colour and surface. Some passages are built slowly, with thin layers that allow earlier marks to remain present; others ask for a more decisive gesture.

## Leaving room for the painting

Working in layers is also a way of listening. A shape may become quieter after another colour is placed beside it, while a small change in light can bring an overlooked detail forward. The process is less about reaching a fixed image quickly and more about recognising when the work has found its own balance.

> Sometimes the most important decision is to leave a little space untouched.

A new group of paintings is taking shape around these ideas. The next studio notes will follow how the works develop from their first marks into more settled compositions.

## A closer look

The afternoon brings together paintings at different stages of completion. Seeing them side by side offers a chance to notice how a recurring colour, a softened edge, or an open area of canvas can change the feeling of an entire composition.

The gathering is informal and designed for conversation. No prior knowledge of painting is needed; curiosity and time to look are enough.

Memory rarely arrives as a complete picture. It is more likely to return as a particular warmth, a muted blue, or the feeling of light at the end of a day. These fragments can be more suggestive than a detailed recollection.

## A palette of impressions

In the studio, a remembered colour is not copied so much as translated. It changes beside other colours, gathers new associations, and gradually becomes part of a present-tense image.

Painting offers a way to stay with these impressions without needing to explain them completely. The viewer can bring their own memories to the work and let the colours open a different conversation.

<!-- entry:journal-studio-light-study -->
Light in the studio is never quite the same from one hour to the next. A colour that feels settled in the morning may become unexpectedly vivid later in the day.

Returning to a painting under different conditions is a useful reminder that looking is an active part of making. It can reveal where a transition needs patience and where a stronger contrast gives the composition its rhythm.

These observations continue to inform the current group of works, especially the quieter passages where small tonal differences carry much of the atmosphere.

<!-- entry:journal-story-space-between -->
A painting is shaped by what is placed on the surface and by what is allowed to remain open. The intervals between marks can hold a surprising amount of energy.

Sometimes a pause gives the eye a place to rest. Sometimes it makes a nearby gesture feel more immediate. These spaces are not empty in the ordinary sense; they are part of the composition's quiet structure.

## Looking back

The conversations around the paintings returned often to the experience of slowing down. Visitors noticed details at different distances, and each change in viewpoint offered another way into the work.

The selection remains an important point of reference for the ongoing studio practice and for the questions that continue to shape new paintings.

A limited palette can make small differences feel more important. Warm neutrals create a steady ground, while a measured note of blue or green can shift the mood without disturbing the overall balance.

The study began as a way to test these relationships before they find their place in larger compositions. It remains open, with several possibilities still being considered.
